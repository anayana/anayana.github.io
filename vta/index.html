Reports

Who it is for, in which columns, and in which file. The head is typed once; the columns come from the client's own blank form.

Letter head

This is what stands above every report: the two addresses, the job, the date and place, and who signs it.

Signature

Drawn with a finger, kept on this phone, and set above the line in the page, the Word file and the long report.

This is a picture of a signature – the same thing a signature on paper is, worth what the process around it is worth. It is not a qualified electronic signature: one of those needs a certificate issued to a named person by a trust service provider on a card or a signing service, and no web page on a phone can make one. What the app can do instead is the checksum: the finished report is hashed with SHA-256 and the hash printed on it, so a changed figure gives a different number. That is a seal against silent alteration and says nothing about who signed.

Template

Write it

The page opens in a new tab: print it, and the phone's own print dialogue writes the PDF. Word opens the .docx straight from the download.

The long report

Every tree on its own, with its measurements, its history and its verdict – not a table. Opens as a page to print.

One HTML file with the trees in it – opens in any browser, can be mailed or put on a web server as it is.

Map sheets to print

The scale is chosen first and the area is cut into as many sheets as that scale needs – a job squeezed onto one page is unreadable and no use to anybody in a park. Every tile is written into the file, so the sheets print and mail without a signal.

1:1000 is the largest scale offered because the map tiles stop at zoom 19: below that the same pixels are only stretched, which is a bigger blur and not more detail. The dots and the numbers are drawn by the printer, not by the tiles, so they stay sharp at every scale.

↓Bring trees in

Import from a file, a city register, OpenStreetMap or an address – and the services that name them.

Get trees

One list for everything: a file on the phone, OpenStreetMap, a city's own register, or any address you paste. The columns of whatever arrives are shown for confirmation before a single tree is added.

Adding keeps what you already have: it brings in trees this phone does not know and fills in blanks, never deletes, and where a value differs yours is kept and the difference reported. Replacing throws the register away first – your own field records are kept and re-attached by tree number.

A register gives positions to metre level: which tree is which, not where the stem is. Stand at each stem and survey it properly. A service that refuses browser requests is named as such – then open the address in a browser, save the file, and import it as a file.

Identification service

Nature Identification API by Observation.org and Naturalis. Without a token the public endpoint allows ten identifications a day. Suggestions are never recorded on their own.

Pl@ntNet

Species from several organs at once – leaf, flower, fruit, bark, the whole tree – the way Tree-Quest does it. Free for non-commercial use with a daily quota; the key is yours and stays on this phone. In a tree's Photos tab, Pl@ntNet sends up to five of its pictures together.

↑Give data out

Export, the research bundle, and putting your own trees on OpenStreetMap.

Export

Give the measurements to research

Two things here are hard to come by and you produce them anyway: a photograph labelled with the organ on it and the species of the tree you were standing at, and a depth-camera slice of a trunk next to the diameter a tape gave for the same stem. The first trains identification, the second scores measuring a stem by phone. Switched off until you switch it on, and nothing is ever sent anywhere by itself – it is a file you save and give to whom you choose, under CC BY 4.0. In it: the tree, its position, species, size, the labelled pictures and the stem scans. Not in it: your name unless you type it here, the remarks, the verdicts, the traffic-safety findings, and nothing at all out of a register you imported. Stem scans are kept only while the switch is on.

Upload to OpenStreetMap

Only trees this phone recorded on site go up, and only the tree itself – species, girth, height, crown, year. Findings and verdicts stay here. A register you imported is never uploaded: its licence is the city's to give, not yours. Each tree is checked against what is already mapped within three metres, and a run is capped at fifty trees, because more than that is an import in OSM's sense and wants the import guidelines first. Setting it up once: on openstreetmap.org, under My Settings → OAuth 2 applications, register this page – redirect URI is this address without anything after it, permissions read user preferences and modify the map – then paste the client ID here.

◌Who is using this

The people on this phone and what each of them may do.

Users

With no users the phone is one person's and asks nothing. Create one and it asks who is holding it; every change is then stamped with that name and the time and written to the trail below. A viewer reads and changes nothing; an inspector records; an admin also imports, replaces, deletes and manages users. The PIN keeps a colleague from saving under your name – the lock screen keeps everyone else out.

⚙Settings

Language, voice, the switches, and what the session can see.

Language

One setting for everything: what the fields are called, what the drop-downs say, the method text, and which language the phone speaks and listens in. The same switch sits in the top bar and on the microphone bar, so it can be changed in the field without coming here.

The values underneath are stored in one vocabulary whatever the language, so a record spoken in English and shown in Estonian is the same record. Speaking is never limited to the chosen language – the words of all five are in the parser at once, so „damage class moderate“ is understood by an Estonian form and stored as the value it shows as keskmine. Remarks stay in the language they were spoken in.

· Nederlands, Eesti and Suomi are translated but have not been read by anyone who speaks them, and the method text in those languages is still English.

App

Standing at a known tree and pressing I am here opens its form and starts the microphone straight away, because at the trunk both hands are busy. Switch that off here if you would rather start it yourself.

A tree is recorded from where you stand, which is rarely the stem. On recording, the app asks how far away the trunk is and moves it there, along the way the camera is pointing or, outside AR, along the compass.

After an entry is made the form scrolls that line to the top edge, so the answered questions leave the screen and the next open ones are in front of you.

Voice

The 🎤 on a tree's page listens. Say „Baum 51" and it opens the tree and reads the species; then it asks the open fields one by one – „Vitalität zwei", „Schadklasse gering", „Verkehrssicherheit gegeben", „Dringlichkeit sofort", „Bemerkung …" – and reads back what it took. „Weiter" skips, „Foto" takes one, „Speichern", „Stop". On Chrome for Android the recogniser runs on Google's servers, so it needs a signal; the bar says so when there is none.

Step in front of the stem

When neither depth nor the ring has the trunk, a recorded tree is put this far ahead along the direction you are facing – the step you stand back from a stem. The session knows that direction to a fraction of a degree; the compass is not used for it. Set 0 to record exactly where you stand.

●State of the app

What is stored, how the diameter is read off a stem, and the page that repairs a broken app.

Status

If this app ever refuses to start – on this phone or on somebody else's – there is a page outside it that saves the data, clears the stored programme copy and puts the data back: anayana.github.io/vta-repair.html. Worth a bookmark before it is needed; it cannot be reached from in here when the app is the thing that is broken.

Measuring the diameter by phone

With depth switched on, Tools → DBH — walk the stem reads the diameter the way a caliper pair is meant to approximate it: not twice at right angles, but all the way round.

  1. Stand about one metre from the bark. Closer than half a metre the trunk fills the frame; past two and a half the sampling is too coarse for bark.
  2. Hold the phone at 1.30 m, level. On a slope, start on the uphill side – that is where the diameter is defined.
  3. Walk slowly round the tree at that distance. The bar counts the degrees actually seen.
  4. Stop at 270° or more. One standing view already shows about 158° of a 50 cm stem, but the far side is then inferred rather than measured.

Expect the reading to sit within roughly 1.5 to 2.3 cm of a caliper on a regular stem. Forks, buttresses, ivy, deep fluting and stem sprouts break the assumption that the outline is a circle: the fit reports how far the surface sits off round and refuses rather than guessing. Every value recorded this way carries how it was measured and over what arc, so it is never mistaken for a caliper reading in the file.

Depth measures the trunk in front of the phone: the marker goes on the stem instead of where you stand, the diameter comes with it, and the session aligns itself on the stems it sees. It runs a second pipeline inside ARCore, which not every phone survives – so it is off until you switch it on. If a session dies a few seconds after starting, switch it off again and everything else keeps working.
