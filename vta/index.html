Give the measurements to research

Two things here are hard to come by and you produce them anyway: a photograph labelled with the organ on it and the species of the tree you were standing at, and a depth-camera slice of a trunk next to the diameter a tape gave for the same stem. The first trains identification, the second scores measuring a stem by phone. Switched off until you switch it on, and nothing is ever sent anywhere by itself – it is a file you save and give to whom you choose, under CC BY 4.0. In it: the tree, its position, species, size, the labelled pictures and the stem scans. Not in it: your name unless you type it here, the remarks, the verdicts, the traffic-safety findings, and nothing at all out of a register you imported. Stem scans are kept only while the switch is on.

Trees from OpenStreetMap

Real trees, downloaded from OpenStreetMap, with whatever species, girth, height and crown somebody recorded. This is the one tree dataset that is open by licence in every country, and it is reachable from a phone in a park. It is not complete: it holds what people have mapped, which in a well-surveyed park is most of the trees and on an ordinary street is often none. What you download is under ODbL 1.0, © OpenStreetMap contributors; the licence is stored on every tree and rides along into exports. A downloaded tree is never offered back to OSM as new.

Upload to OpenStreetMap

Only trees this phone recorded on site go up, and only the tree itself – species, girth, height, crown, year. Findings and verdicts stay here. A register you imported is never uploaded: its licence is the city's to give, not yours. Each tree is checked against what is already mapped within three metres, and a run is capped at fifty trees, because more than that is an import in OSM's sense and wants the import guidelines first. Setting it up once: on openstreetmap.org, under My Settings → OAuth 2 applications, register this page – redirect URI is this address without anything after it, permissions read user preferences and modify the map – then paste the client ID here.

Import

Merging adds trees this phone does not have and fills in what it is missing. It never deletes, and where a value differs, yours is kept and the difference reported.

Load from a web address

Paste the address of an ArcGIS layer, a WFS GetFeature request, or a GeoJSON or CSV file. An ArcGIS layer address is completed to a query by itself and read in pages. The columns are shown for confirmation before anything is imported. A server that does not allow browser requests is named as such – then open the address in the browser, save the file, and use Merge a register….

Berlin tree register

The city's open register of street and park trees. Positions in it are metre-level – it says which tree is which, not where the stem is.

Identification service

Nature Identification API by Observation.org and Naturalis. Without a token the public endpoint allows ten identifications a day. Suggestions are never recorded on their own.

App

A tree is recorded from where you stand, which is rarely the stem. On recording, the app asks how far away the trunk is and moves it there, along the way the camera is pointing or, outside AR, along the compass.

After an entry is made the form scrolls that line to the top edge, so the answered questions leave the screen and the next open ones are in front of you.

Language

What the fields are called and what the drop-downs say. The values underneath are stored in one vocabulary whatever the language, so a record spoken in English and shown in Estonian is the same record. English is always one tap away.

Voice

Speak English to an Estonian form: "damage class moderate" is stored as the value and shown as keskmine. Remarks stay in the language they were spoken in.

The 🎤 on a tree's page listens. Say „Baum 51" and it opens the tree and reads the species; then it asks the open fields one by one – „Vitalität zwei", „Schadklasse gering", „Verkehrssicherheit gegeben", „Dringlichkeit sofort", „Bemerkung …" – and reads back what it took. „Weiter" skips, „Foto" takes one, „Speichern", „Stop". On Chrome for Android the recogniser runs on Google's servers, so it needs a signal; the bar says so when there is none.

When neither depth nor the ring has the trunk, a recorded tree is put this far ahead along the direction you are facing – the step you stand back from a stem. The session knows that direction to a fraction of a degree; the compass is not used for it. Set 0 to record exactly where you stand.

Measuring the diameter by phone

With depth switched on, Tools → DBH — walk the stem reads the diameter the way a caliper pair is meant to approximate it: not twice at right angles, but all the way round.

  1. Stand about one metre from the bark. Closer than half a metre the trunk fills the frame; past two and a half the sampling is too coarse for bark.
  2. Hold the phone at 1.30 m, level. On a slope, start on the uphill side – that is where the diameter is defined.
  3. Walk slowly round the tree at that distance. The bar counts the degrees actually seen.
  4. Stop at 270° or more. One standing view already shows about 158° of a 50 cm stem, but the far side is then inferred rather than measured.

Expect the reading to sit within roughly 1.5 to 2.3 cm of a caliper on a regular stem. Forks, buttresses, ivy, deep fluting and stem sprouts break the assumption that the outline is a circle: the fit reports how far the surface sits off round and refuses rather than guessing. Every value recorded this way carries how it was measured and over what arc, so it is never mistaken for a caliper reading in the file.

Depth measures the trunk in front of the phone: the marker goes on the stem instead of where you stand, the diameter comes with it, and the session aligns itself on the stems it sees. It runs a second pipeline inside ARCore, which not every phone survives – so it is off until you switch it on. If a session dies a few seconds after starting, switch it off again and everything else keeps working.
